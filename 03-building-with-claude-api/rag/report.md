# Northwind Labs — Annual Review 2026

Northwind Labs runs two divisions under one roof: a medical research group and a software
engineering group. This review covers both, along with finances and the risks ahead.

## Medical Research

The research group spent the year on gut-microbiome studies. Dr. Okafor's team traced a
recurring stomach bug in the trial cohort to a single contaminated reagent batch. Once the
batch was replaced, infection rates in the cohort fell from 9% to under 1%. The team
published four papers, e.g. on how antibiotic timing shifts recovery. Two more are under
review.

## Software Engineering

The engineering group shipped the Atlas scheduling platform to 40 hospital customers.
Engineers fixed 1,240 bugs this year, down from 1,910 the year before. Most of the drop
came from a new code review rule: every change touching patient data needs two approvals.
Median time to fix a critical bug fell from 6 days to 2.

## Financial Performance

Revenue reached $48.2M, up 31% year over year. Atlas licences made up 70% of it; research
grants made up the rest. Operating costs rose 18%, mostly from hiring 35 engineers. The
company closed the year with $22M in cash.

## Risk Factors

Three risks stand out. First, 60% of Atlas revenue comes from five customers, so losing
one would hurt. Second, the research group depends on a single grant renewal due in March.
Third, new health-data regulation in the EU could require changes to how Atlas stores
records.

## Outlook

Next year the company plans to take Atlas to Europe and to start a second microbiome
trial. Hiring will slow to 15 engineers. The board expects revenue growth of 20 to 25%.
